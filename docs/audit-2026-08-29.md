# No-fabrication audit — 29 August 2026

Three passages of new or rewritten chapter text were checked against the research files
before they were allowed to stand on the site: the new Klimontów chapter, the rewritten
sections of the Volhynia/Liben chapter, and two new sections of the Wojsławice town
chapter.

## Method

Each was given to an independent checker with one instruction: **assume the writer erred,
and try to prove it.** The checker was given the permitted source files and told that a
claim counts as unsupported unless it can be quoted from one of them — and that stating
something *more confidently than its source does* is as serious as inventing it, because
a reader cannot tell the difference.

That second rule is what most of the findings turned out to be.

## Result

**21 problems across the two chapters audited first — 7 unsupported, 14 overstated. All
were corrected before the text stayed on the site.** The most serious:

| What the draft said | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Houses 99 (1847) and 309 as evidence of a second household | 99 and 309 are two **rival readings of the same act**, 12/1847. The draft had manufactured a person out of a palaeographic disagreement. |
| "All ten Volodymyr volumes were swept … none between 1859 and 1865" | **1860, sprava 280, was never downloaded** — and 1860 is exactly the year between. A finding of 23 August exists specifically to retract that claim. |
| "Tanchum **Szmuelowicz** Liben", graded ● | «шмоилово» is **unresolved**, graded ◐◐, and the sources explicitly refuse to enter a Szmuel in the tree on one word in one register. |
| "The elder Hercyk died … and Ruchla died" | **Acts 34 and 53 were never read.** Only act 40 has been. Both attributions are ◐. |
| "within weeks" | The evidence gives **four months**. |
| "a town in the Sandomierz district" | Klimontów is in the **Radom gubernia**. Sandomierz is the archive's name. |
| "eleven Rywenbajrych birth acts" | The roster itself lists **twelve**. |
| Cholera as the explanation for the 1867 deaths | **No document in the file says it.** Kept, but marked ◌ and labelled a reading, not a finding. |
| "Josek Bornsztejn" | **Bursztyn.** |
| "the town's name stayed open almost a year" | **Eighteen days.** |

## What this says about the sources

Three arithmetic errors were found not in the writing but **in the findings files
themselves**, and had been copied faithfully into the draft:

- "nine readings" where the table listed seven — nine *acts* were read, two of which
  yielded nothing.
- "four Rywenbajrych deaths in 1867" where the full census lists three.
- "sixty-eight years" between the 1838 and 1903 acts, which are sixty-five apart — on a
  page whose own blockquote turns on `1838 + 65 = 1903`.

A number that appears in a finding is not thereby verified. Two of these three surfaced
only because translators compared counts against the tables beside them.

## One defect left standing, deliberately

`@I6@` — the elder Hercyk — carries `1 FAMC @F1@`, so the tree files him as a son of
Rywen and Łaca and therefore as Izrael's brother. His own note says that parentage is
"inference only" and that no document states it. Any tree view or export will render an
unproven brotherhood as a fact.

This was **not** changed. Altering the shape of the tree is the family's decision, not
the editor's.

## Standing rule

Every factual sentence added to a chapter cites the findings file it came from, in an
HTML comment or a footnote. Where a source leaves a question open, the chapter leaves it
open in the same words; where two sources disagree, both readings appear. The grades
● documented, ◐ probable and ○ inference carry across from the research files unchanged,
and a grade is never raised in the retelling.
