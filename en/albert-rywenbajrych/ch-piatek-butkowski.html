Albert–Rywenbajrych · Chapter 2

Piątek: the Butkowski family

The family of Rudla, the bride of Dawid Alpert, came from a small market town 134 kilometres from Warsaw. Three of its generations opened at once — after it emerged that a single letter in the marriage act had been misread for months.

●● documented — two acts read from the scans, corroborated by the JRI index Source: fond 52/135/0, Płock Archive, Łęczyca branch

The letter that was misread

For months the bride's name in marriage act 75/1894 was read as "Buzowska" (Бужовская), and every search on that name — in every database — returned nothing. The solution was not a new search method but a re-reading of the act itself, at eight times magnification from the original scan1:

“The bride, the maiden Rudla Butkowska, daughter of Abram and Ruchla — twenty-one years of age… of the gmina of Piątek in the Kalisz gubernia, residing temporarily in Warsaw” Невѣста дѣвица Рудля Бутковская дочь Абрама и Рухли — двадцати одного года, …Гмины Пiонтки Калишской губернiи, временно въ Варшавѣ проживающая. Warsaw Archive, Cyrkuł V, marriage act 75/1894.

The third letter is т with the long horizontal bar above it, not ж. And the moment it was corrected, the whole chain of spellings fell into place with nothing left over — including the garbled "Gutkowska" on the CKŻP card of 1945 and "Budkowska" in the 2015 citizenship application1. The name is Butkowski / Budkowski, and the two forms alternate under the same clerk in the very same register.

In the Piątek register for 1883 the clerk took the trouble to write the name in brackets in Latin letters as well: „(Butkowski)”. There is no room for doubt.

Three generations in Piątek

Berek Budkowski ⚭ Rudel Szydłowicz — married Piątek 1833, act 6; he d. 1852
Abram Butkowski ⚭ Ruchla Rochberger — married Piątek 1864, act 6; he b. 1842, she b. 1844
Rudla Butkowska — b. Piątek 1872, act 22; married in Warsaw 1894

Abram Butkowski was born in Piątek in 1842, and in 1864 married Ruchla Rochberger, born in Piątek in 1844. Her maiden name — which the Warsaw act of 1894 did not trouble to record — is well documented in the Piątek registers themselves, where the Rochberger family appears again and again as a local family2. Two acts from the Piątek birth register were read from the scans, and they also record the change in Abram's standing:

Between 1879 and 1883 Abram moved up from labourer to tailor — a skilled trade. The same two acts also appear in the JRI index, which was found entirely separately, and the two confirm each other2.

Rudla's siblings

Abram and Ruchla had six documented children in Piątek, Rudla the second of them2: Sura-Gaja (1869), Rudla (1872), Maria (1875), Szaja (1878 — died that same year in infancy), Szulim/Szmul (25 November 1879) and Chaja (4 April 1883).

The generation above them, and the name that corroborates it

Above Abram stand Berek Budkowski and Rudel Szydłowicz, who married in Piątek in 1833; Berek died there in 1852. The sequence of births in the register — Mendel-Szaja (1834), Malka (1836), Frajda (1839), Abram (1842), Icyk (1845) — places Abram as their son2. This link is rated probable, not documented: Abram's own birth act has not yet been read, and it is the one that will name his parents.

What strengthens it is a name: Rudel Szydłowicz, the bride of 1833, and Rudla, born 1872 — a rare name, identical, skipping one generation. This is the classic Ashkenazi naming signature, and in the absence of an act it is the strongest evidence for the link3.

Why this chapter was stuck for so long

Three traps stood in the way, and all of them are recorded here so that whoever continues will not fall into them:

  • A duplicated town name. "Piątek Wielki" is an entirely different town, Catholic, near Kalisz. Our Piątek is catalogued under the Płock voivodeship, because the catalogue uses the administrative division of 1975–1998; the act says "Kalisz gubernia" because the Łęczyca district belonged to that tsarist gubernia4.
  • An index that does not answer a town search. The JRI dataset for Piątek exists and is nearly complete — births 1826–1903, marriages 1826–1904, deaths 1826–1891 — but a search by town name returns nothing. It responds only to a search by surname, within the Kalisz gubernia bucket2.
  • Two databases that hold nothing. Geneteka and Skanoteka do not contain the Jewish registers of Piątek at all — checked against the full listings, not by spot search5.
