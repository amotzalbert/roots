Albert–Rywenbajrych · Chapter 3

Apta: the Rywenbajrych and Wajsblum families

Opatów — which the Hasidim called "Apta", the seat of R. Avraham Yehoshua Heschel, the "Ohev Yisrael" — is the maternal side of this branch. From there came Tuvia Rywenbajrych and his wife Bajla-Szajndla Wajsblum, the parents of Chana, Amotz's grandmother.

◐ probable — the Opatów registers are not scanned; the identification rests on the JRI-Poland index Sources: acts 24/1884, 8/1892 (index only) · Yad Vashem Pages of Testimony

A Hasidic town in Congress Poland

Opatów, in the district of the same name, was a market town where Jews and Poles lived together for centuries; among the Hasidim it was known as "Apta" — the seat of R. Avraham Yehoshua Heschel (1748–1825), author of Ohev Yisrael and one of the leading rabbis of his generation. Iwaniska and Klimontów, two nearby towns where earlier generations of the family lived, were subordinate to Opatów both communally and administratively1. For the Jews of Iwaniska itself, for instance, no separate register survived — their marriages were recorded in the Opatów registers, in which eighteen couples from Iwaniska can be traced2.

What has been read, and what is still only an index

The Jewish civil registers of Opatów itself — births, marriages, deaths — are not scanned in any free database that has been located, and the whole identification of Bajla-Szajndla Wajsblum and of her parents rests on JRI-Poland index lines alone, and is therefore rated ◐ probable. But another, separate document — the register of non-permanent residents of Opatów, 1921 — was scanned, and has been read in full at full magnification: in it Tuvia himself, then about thirty-three, gave his parents' names before the registration clerk. A son's own testimony about his parents, given at the time and entered in an official document, is documentation of a high order — not hearsay and not third-generation family memory3.

Tuvia Rywenbajrych ⚭ Bajla-Szajndla Wajsblum — b. c. 1888, Iwaniska. Both were murdered in the Holocaust; the memorial list (1988): Treblinka, 23 October 1942
Chana Rywenbajrych — b. 18 June 1917, Opatów. Survived the Holocaust

Tuvia, and Abe-Lejb his father

Tuvia Rywenbajrych was born in Iwaniska around 1888, the son of Abe-Lejb and Fajga-Rachel Rywenbajrych — as he himself stated in the register of non-permanent residents of Opatów, 19213. The same entry gives four further details: he was a merchant (kupiec); his formal place of belonging remained the gmina of Iwaniska; his last place of residence was Opatów; and the family was registered at a house at Drewska 11. The name is written there as Rubinbajrach — a fourth spelling, which was not on the list of spellings being searched, and that is why all the indexes missed the family3. "Abe" in the Latin spelling is almost certainly short for Abram or Abraham — which of the two is not resolved; and Fajga-Rachel's maiden name was not recorded in any source found — the maiden-name column is empty in his line, while in the lines of his daughters it was filled in. An indirect supporting sign: the daughter born in 1920 was named by Tuvia Fajga-Rachel — his mother's name. By the Ashkenazi naming custom, this suggests that his mother was no longer alive in 19203. Abe-Lejb and Fajga-Rachel themselves — although they are mentioned on Geni, the family database that preceded this site — were entirely absent from the GEDCOM and from the research data until the gap was noticed and corrected4.

Bajla-Szajndla, of the Wajsblum family

Tuvia's wife, Bajla-Szajndla Wajsblum, born in Opatów in 1892, is identified from birth act 8/1892 (index only — the act itself has not been read) and from two Pages of Testimony at Yad Vashem5.

⚠ And on the identity of her parents there is a contradiction that has not been resolved. The Opatów index assigns birth act 8/1892 to Moszek Wajsblum and Gela née Ginsburg, a couple who also appear in marriage act 24/1884 and in the necrology of the yizkor book6. But the 1921 population register, read directly from the scan, names her parents as Rafał and Fajga3. The two sources cannot be reconciled, and the Opatów index batch for the years after 1887 does not include the parents' columns at all — so no decision is possible without reading act 8/1892 itself. One hint does support the 1921 version: the name Rafał did indeed occur in the Wajsblum family of Opatów in those years, and the index shows a Wajsblum Rafał and a Rafał-Tobiasz, both born in 18876.

Chana and Fajga-Rachel, the daughters

Tuvia and Bajla-Szajndla had two documented daughters: Chana, on 18 June 1917, and Fajga-Rachel, around 1920, both in Opatów3. The younger daughter carried her grandmother's name, Fajga-Rachel — a name that recurs across two generations in the same family. Chana is the one who married Chaim Albert — see the next chapter; she survived the Holocaust. Fajga-Rachel, her younger sister, was murdered — shot dead at the Skarżysko-Kamienna camp, according to a Page of Testimony at Yad Vashem that Chana herself submitted for her sister7. Chaim Albert, according to his registration card, was a prisoner in the same camp in 1942 — approximately the same year. Whether he and Chana were there at the same time is unknown7.
