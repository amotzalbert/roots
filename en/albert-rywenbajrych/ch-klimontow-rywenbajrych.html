Albert–Rywenbajrych · Chapter 7

Klimontów: the Rywenbajrych line, and one word across nine acts

In one small town in the Radom gubernia stands the most certain line in this branch — three generations, the last of them locked between a birth act and a death act. And in that same town stood the word that resisted reading more stubbornly than any other word in the file, until a sixfold enlargement showed that the letter we had read as m was a w.

● Documented in the lowest link · ◐ in the generation above it — and open questions, recorded as such Source: the Klimontów registers, AP Sandomierz, through GenBaza and JRI-Poland

The line of fathers

The surname explains itself. Rywenbajrych — Rywen-bajrych derives from the given name Reuven, and was most likely formed around the surname legislation of the Congress Kingdom of Poland of 1821, exactly as happened to the Cukier family at Chęciny in those same years1. And the Reuven from whom the name derives stands at the head of the line.

Rywen and Łaca — the deepest generation here, born about 1785–1795 by the reckoning of their son's age. They have no act of their own; they are named in a single document only, the death act of their son, and so are known by their given names alone. “Łaca” is probably Leah, or Laja. This couple rests on that one act alone, and in the tree itself it is marked “probable” and not “documented” — after a reading that made it out to be more than that was checked and withdrawn.

⚠ And a warning recorded with the name: marriage act 4/1834 gives Jeremiasz Rywenbajrych a mother called “Łaji”, and Jeremiasz's father is Hercyk, not Rywen. Which means either that there were two couples in the town whose wife was called Łaja/Łaca, or that the two men are bound up with each other in a way not yet deciphered. ◌ Open.

Izrael Rywenbajrych, a trader, was born about 1815–1819 — the act of 1838 makes him twenty-three and the act of his death forty-eight, and the two testimonies do not quite coincide. He died on 3 September 1867 at Klimontów, at twelve noon, and the act calls him “syn Rywena i Łacy” — son of Rywen and Łaca. He left a widow — the act calls her Maja-Rejzla, and two other documents call her Chaja, which is the form the file adopted2.

Hercyk Rywenbajrych, his son, was born on 17 September 1838 at Klimontów; the act was registered eight days later, and the name was given at the circumcision. It was his father Izrael who declared the birth, and signed for himself — in Latin letters and in Hebrew. Hercyk died in 1903, aged 65, and his death act calls him the son of Izrael and Chaja-Rejza, and leaves a widow named Tauba-Cyrla.

1838 + 65 = 1903. The birth act and the death act lock onto each other: the same name, the same parents' names, the same age. This is the securest link in the branch.

A second household, and all the children in it

Not every Rywenbajrych at Klimontów belongs to this line. A second Hercyk lived in the town, a much older man — born about 1812 — and to him and his wife Ruchla children were born almost without a break between 1845 and 1867. Every Rywenbajrych birth act at Klimontów in those years belongs to this household, and not one of them to Izrael3.

He moved between trades: a trader in pots in 1845, an oil trader in 1847, and plain “trader” in 1854 and in 1861. The house numbers in the registers vary as well — 122 in 1852 — and for some of them the reading itself is contested: for act 12/1847 the file records two readings, 99 and 309, and for act 27/1854 two, 105 and 605. ⚠ These are alternative readings of the same figure in the same act, not two houses and not two men.

Six of them did not reach the age of three. This is no anomaly in a Jewish register in nineteenth-century Poland, and it can be seen here gathered in one place only because every act of this household was read.

Maior, the child who filled the gap

The sequence of births in this household keeps an almost regular rhythm — a birth roughly every two years. And when the series was first built, a jump of seven years yawned between 1847 and 1854, with no explanation.

Death act 14/1850 filled it:

“died MAIOR RYWENBAJRYCH, six months old, son of HERCYK and RUCHLA née LEWEK RYWENBAJRYCH, traders resident at Klimontów” umarł MAIOR RYWENBAJRYCH, pół roku mający, syn CHERCYKA i RUCHLI z LEWKÓW RYWENBAJRYCHÓW, handlarzy w Klimontowie zamieszkałych

He died on 22 September 1850, at two in the morning, and by the reckoning of his age was born around March of that year. His birth act has not yet been located. Maior and his sister Szendla, born in April 1852, fill the gap exactly4.

One word, nine acts

Ruchla's maiden name was the hard palaeographic problem of this file. Nine birth acts of this household were read in the original, and in every one of them stood that same word, across twenty-two years. In one — 12/1847 — I could not read it at all; in another — 25/1858 — the maiden name is not given. The remaining seven gave seven answers:

Two candidates competed — Lemkowicz/Lemel against Kimet — and the file passed through two readings that were adopted and then withdrawn. The note left in the GEDCOM admitted as much outright: “I adopted a reading twice, and twice had to take it back. This remains open.”

What settled it was a sixfold enlargement of act 27/1854:

The third letter is a single-humped w, not an m. The reading is z LEWKOWICZÓW, not Lemkowiczów.

And from there everything falls into place. LEWKÓW and LEWKOWICZÓW are one and the same patronymic, in a short form and a long one: Ruchla, daughter of Lewek. The seven readings collapse into one, and act 14/1850 — read the same day — supplies the direct witness, since the same woman is called by both forms in the same registers.

This solution settled a second question as well. In act 19/1852 Hercyk's wife is called Rywka and not Ruchla, and that was enough for a duplicate record to appear in the tree. But “z Lewków” recurs in both acts: the maiden name is the constant, and only the given name slipped, once. The two duplicate records were merged — and not deleted, but kept as empty shells with the documentation, so that the error and its correction remain legible.

⚠ And what was not done. The Chęciny branch has a “Lewek” of its own — the father of Ester Potasznicka. No link whatever was made between the two. A different town, a different generation, and one of the commonest given names there is. Joining them would have built a generation on top of an unproven couple, and the line was stopped at the place where the evidence stops.

1867 — the year that erased both households

In a single year Klimontów registered three Rywenbajrych deaths and one birth: Necha was born in April; and in that same year death act 34 (the elder Hercyk), act 40 (Izrael, in September) and act 53 (Ruchla) were registered. The infant Necha died in 1869.

⚠ And of the three, only act 40 has been read in the original. Acts 34 and 53 are so far known from an index line alone; their attribution to Hercyk and to Ruchla is ◐ and not ●, and we have no date for either of them. What can be said with certainty is that in one register, in one year, three deaths under this surname were recorded in a small town — and that the baby girl born in April was left an orphan within that same year.

The two witnesses who reported Necha's birth in April, Abram Sandomierski and Moszek Agater, are the same two men who reported Izrael's death four months later. The same two neighbours, the same family, twice in the same year.

◌ And what begs to be concluded and cannot be: three adult deaths in one year look like the signature of an epidemic, and 1867 is indeed reckoned a year of cholera outbreak in Russian Poland. But no document in the file says so. The one act that has been read gives no cause of death, and the other two have not been read at all. This is a plausible reading of an accumulation, not a finding.

What is still open

Act 12/1847 is the most open of them. In the August reading round it was recorded that I could not read the maiden-name word in it at all; in other rounds a third form was read from it, “z Sternebugów” or “z Sternebergów” — German in character, and not Lewek at all — and in the GEDCOM itself no maiden name is recorded for this act whatsoever. Three testimonies, three different results, and one and the same act. ⇒ The word remains undecided, and is recorded as such. Settling it requires a fresh reading of the original act at maximum enlargement.

The maiden name of Chaja-Rejzla, Izrael's wife, has not been settled. The birth act of 1838 — the one her husband himself declared — reads “z Maszklow”, in a reading that is not certain; her son's death act of 1903, sixty-five years later, says Zalcberg. The weight of testimony ought to favour the contemporary document, but the palaeography there is loose, and a Zalcberg family does exist at Klimontów in those years — Chaskel Zalcberg, a trader aged 23, testifies in act 19/1852. The balance now leans to Zalcberg, but the grade stays ◐.

And two reading tasks stand open: Maior's birth act of 1850, which would give his exact day, and death acts 34/1867 and 53/1867, of Hercyk and Ruchla — which have still not been read in the original, and whose grade is therefore ◐ and not ●.

◌ And above all there stands a hypothesis that has not been proved: that the elder Hercyk is Izrael's brother, that is, another son of Rywen and Łaca. Two traders of the same surname, in the same small town, of the same generation. The naming pattern supports it — Chaja-Laja, born in 1847, carries Łaca's name, and the index holds a death of “Laja Rywenbajrych” in 1835 — but one difficulty remains untouched: Izrael named his son Hercyk in 1838 while the elder Hercyk was alive, and naming a child after a living relative is unusual. It may be that both were named after a Hercyk who died in 1836. It may be that they are not brothers at all. Not documented, and therefore not asserted.

And what was ruled out

This chapter opened on an entirely different question: where Tuvia Rywenbajrych was born. The complete birth indexes of Klimontów for the years 1888–1892 were read in the original — 183 births, each list with its closing protocol, that is, complete and not partial — and in none of them is there a Rywenbajrych. And JRI indexes the Klimontów births for the years 1826–1887, and in all those years there are twelve Rywenbajrych birth acts in all, and no Tuvia among them.

There is no birth act for Tuvia Rywenbajrych at Klimontów between 1826 and 1892. ● A documented negative.

Together with the absence of any Rywenbajrych from the Opatów index, this strengthens the reading that Chana's father was a Wajsblum and not a Rywenbajrych — or, alternatively, that Tuvia was born in a third town. A negative result is not a failure: it keeps the next search from going down the same road.
