Moskal–Schafir · Chapter 7

Volhynia: the Liben house

Two hundred and fifty kilometres from Wojsławice, in an entirely different city — the capital of an ancient principality in the Russian gubernia of Volhynia — Perl Liben was born, who married Menachem Mendel Schafir and so joined a third branch to the chain. This is the thinnest branch in archival documentation — but not in documentation as such: the three members of this generation are named in a Page of Testimony filled in by Malka, Perl's daughter.

● Documented — Page of Testimony 572892, in the handwriting of Malka Moskal ○ Inference — the generation above, from a birth register of 1865 (ДАВО, fond 96)

Ludmir, in the eyes of its Jews

Włodzimierz Wołyński — to its Jews: Ludmir — was one of the oldest towns of Volhynia, and formerly the capital of the principality of Volhynia; Jews are mentioned there as early as 11711. In the second half of the nineteenth century this was a large community and an absolute majority in the town: about seventy-three per cent of the population around 1880, some eight thousand residents in all2. The main livelihood was trade in cattle, horses and grain2 — an economy entirely different from that of semi-rural Wojsławice. By 1914 the town already had a branch of "Hibbat Zion" (from the end of the nineteenth century) and a branch of the "Bund" (from 1906) — a modern and active community that went on growing after the war as well: in the twenties and thirties there were already some twenty synagogues in it2.

The single archival document

From the Ukrainian archives themselves only one line has been found that contains the name Liben. It is in the Jewish birth register of Włodzimierz Wołyński for the year 1865, read from a free scan of the archive: act number 4, the birth of a daughter named Yente, to the father Szlioma/Shlomo Liben, a merchant, from the town "Ochana" (the reading is not unambiguous — probably Uchanie, a town in the Lublin gubernia twelve kilometres from Wojsławice), and to the mother Malka3. This is a single document, and there is no certainty that it bears directly on the branch in question — but it is the basis of the inference described below.

Shlomo Liben ⚭ Malka — Włodzimierz Wołyński, daughter born 1865 (○ conjectural attribution only)
Shalom Liben ⚭ Khaia Liben — named in the Page of Testimony filled in by their granddaughter Malka
Perl Schafir née Liben ⚭ Menachem Mendel Schafir — b. around 1888, Włodzimierz Wołyński; murdered in the Chełm ghetto, 1942

Perl, and what is known of her with certainty

Much of what is known about the Liben family comes not from a Ukrainian archive but from a single document written in Petah Tikva: the Page of Testimony that Malka Moskal filled in for her mother, on 7 January 1957, in her own hand. The form gives her name — Perl, and the byname Pnina — her age, fifty-four; her place of birth, Włodzimierz Wołyński; the names of her parents: Shalom and Khaia Liben; her place of residence, Wojsławice; and the circumstances of her death: murdered in the Chełm ghetto, 1942. On the line for the relationship Malka signed "daughter"5.

That is to say, the names of Shalom and Khaia Liben are not an inference — they were given by their granddaughter, who knew them by name, and were recorded in an official document. What is not known about them is everything else: dates of birth, marriage and death, and Khaia's family name before her marriage.

The inference: where the name Malka comes from

The known eldest daughter of Perl and Menachem Mendel Schafir was called Malka — Amotz's grandmother. According to the Ashkenazi naming custom, such a name was usually given in memory of a relative who had died. The single document found for the Liben branch happens to name a couple of parents in which the mother's name is exactly "Malka" — in the same city, and in a generation that fits exactly the estimated age of Shalom and Khaia Liben, Perl's parents3. If the inference is correct, Shlomo and Malka Liben are the parents of Shalom Liben — that is, Perl's grandfather and grandmother, and the great-grandfather and great-grandmother of Malka Moskal, who was named after that same great-grandmother. This is an inference only: there is no direct documentation linking the two couples, no marriage act for Shalom and Khaia has been found, and no birth act for Perl herself has been found that could establish the connection with certainty.

How a family came from Ludmir to Wojsławice

The distance between Włodzimierz Wołyński and Wojsławice is about two hundred and fifty kilometres — far more than any other family connection documented in this branch. Both towns were under the same Russian Empire, but in entirely different administrative gubernias — Volhynia against Lublin. One piece of documentary evidence is relevant: a research site on the history of Wojsławice notes that the town sat on a junction of trade roads that included a direct route between Lublin and Włodzimierz4. There is no direct documentation that this route is what brought Perl and Menachem Mendel to one another — arranged marriages between distant communities were common in the Ashkenazi Jewish world of the Pale of Settlement, through networks of matchmakers and trade connections, with no need whatever for geographical proximity — but this is a documented axis of trade that can explain, at least in part, how members of two communities at such a distance came to know of each other.
