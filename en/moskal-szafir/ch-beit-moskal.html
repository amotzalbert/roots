Moskal–Schafir · Chapter 6

The Moskal house: the shop and the beer house

The Moskal family runs through this branch from Lejba Moskal, born around 1817, down to David Moskal, owner of the business beside the Rynek that the residents of Wojsławice still remembered decades after the destruction.

● Documented — three independent Polish witnesses, and a confiscation record Sources: marriage acts 1878/1903, Archiwum Państwowe w Lublinie · oral testimonies, Grodzka Gate Archive

Four generations down to David Moskal

Lejba Moskal, born around 1817, is the earliest documented generation in the Moskal branch — he himself is not mentioned in any act of his own, but only as the father of Dawid-Szloma in the latter's marriage act — where he and his wife Brandlya née Eil are already described as the late1. Dawid-Szloma Moskal, a tailor aged twenty-three, born in and resident of Kraśniczyn, married on 2 January 1878 Hinda Rajs, aged eighteen, born in Wojsławice, daughter of Gershon Rajs and Dwojra née Szlajm 1. Their son Judka-Lejba Moskal — named, as was customary, after his grandfather Lejba, who was no longer alive in 1878 — married on 11 August 1903, aged twenty-five, Tzvia-Dvora Alspektor, aged eighteen, daughter of Hersz Alspektor and Chaja née Rajs2. Both of David Moskal's grandmothers were, then, born Rajs. Their son David Moskal was born in 1910, and married Malka, daughter of Menachem Mendel Schafir3.

Lejba Moskal ⚭ Brandlya Eil — b. around 1817
Dawid-Szloma Moskal ⚭ Hinda Rajs — b. around 1855 in Kraśniczyn
Judka-Lejba Moskal ⚭ Tzvia-Dvora Alspektor — b. 1878, d. 1942
David Moskal ⚭ Malka Schafir — b. 1910

The Rajs side — two generations opened in August 2026

For many years this branch stopped at Gershon Rajs and his wife Dwojra, two names that appeared only as the parents of Hinda in the act of 1878. On 12 August 2026 their own marriage act was read — act 7 of 1847, Wojsławice20 — and it names the parents on both sides.

On the sixteenth of December 1847, at half past three in the afternoon, the deputy rabbi David London married Gershon Rajs, a bachelor of eighteen, born in Wojsławice, son of Wolf — living — and Lea — no longer alive, to Dwojra Szlajm, a maiden of sixteen, daughter of Szmul — living — and Rypka — deceased. All the families were wyrobnicy, day labourers — the poorest layer in the town. Both fathers were present at the wedding canopy and signed for it themselves.

And so, out of a single line on a form, four people entered the tree who had not been in it before — and Dwojra's family name was corrected.

Until this act her name was written «Dwojra née Sztajn», on the basis of the 1878 act. But her father stood at the wedding canopy and was recorded as «Szmul Szlajm, father of Dwojra Szlajm» — and he signed his name. From this it follows that Szlajm is the name, and that «Sztajn» was a clerk's normalisation to the name common in the town. On the same occasion it also emerged that their ages in the death act of 1871 are wrong by about six years: Gershon was born around 1829 and not 1823, and Dwojra around 1831. And Hinda's own birth act, read on the same day, fixes her date exactly — 8 June 1859, at four in the morning21.

Wolf Rajs ⚭ Lea Rajs — day labourers, later butchers; Wolf was alive in 1847 and in 1851
Gershon Rajs ⚭ Dwojra Szlajm — married 16.12.1847; b. around 1829 and 1831
Hinda Rajs ⚭ Dawid-Szloma Moskal — b. 8.6.1859 in Wojsławice

The other side of that same act — Szmul Szlajm and Rypka, Dwojra's parents — is a further new generation, and a family we did not know at all. Wolf Rajs's death act has still not been found: all the Wojsławice death indexes from 1848 to 1884 have been scanned and there is no Wolf in them22.

Judka-Lejba Moskal died before 1942; his wife Tzvia-Dvora was murdered in the Chełm ghetto that same year, the year the Wojsławice community was liquidated4. The chapter on the Destruction deals with this directly.

"The Moskals' shop"

Three Polish testimonies, recorded in Wojsławice between 2003 and 2004 by local researchers independent of one another, describe the family's business.

Maria Jałuchowska, born 1922, remembered: «Two Jewish women ran eating places — one was called Dyncia, and the other Moskalowa», and added that her father used to «go and drink at Dyncia's and at Moskalowa's»5. Danuta Zińczuk, born 1928, described it explicitly as a beer house: «There was one called Moskal, Moskalowa, who had a beer house»6. And Kazimierz Lackowski, born 1926, remembered the menu as well:

«There was Moskalowa too. She had a house of that kind, where one could also eat» — and in the full printed version of his testimony: «Clerks after work would go to Dyncia's for soup, for fish or for veal… My father was a lover of fish, and she made very good fish, especially in the Jewish style.» Kazimierz Lackowski, quoted in Skrawki Pamięci (2005), p. 26; and in a recorded testimony of 30.1.2003, Grodzka Gate Archive, Lublin.

"Moskalowa" is not a given name — it is a Polish feminine possessive form, "Moskal's wife". A surname that became, on the lips of Polish neighbours, the name of a whole woman.

The business in black and white: 1920–1939

The three Polish testimonies describe the beer house from memory. In August 2026 four printed documents were found that place it on an exact timeline — and show that the family ran it for twenty years, in three trades at once.

1920 — the registration. On 30 July 1920 the official gazette Obwieszczenia Publiczne published the list of businesses entered in the Chełm district register. Entry 322: “‘Lejba Moskal’, beer tavern and tea house, seat in Wojsławice, Chełm district, owner Lejba Moskal” — and the owner is Judka-Lejba himself30.

1929 and 1930 — the directory. The Księga Adresowa Polski, the business directory of the whole country, lists Wojsławice twice. In 1929: “Beer houses: Głowacki L. — Moskal D.”31. By 1930 the family appears on three separate lines — tea houses: Moskal D. · beer houses: Moskal D. · soda water: Moskal Ł.32

The family tree has recorded, for years, that Judka-Lejba was “a shopkeeper — haberdashery, then beer retail, then soda water”. All three trades appear on three separate lines of a printed directory from 1930.

And the “D.” of the beer houses is Dwojra — the same “Moskalowa” of the neighbours’ testimony, and the same owner who will appear in the 1940 confiscation record. The businesses were registered in both their names, in different trades: hers the beer house and the tea house, his the soda water.

1939 — the deletion. On 22 June 1939 Monitor Polski published the decisions of the District Court in Lublin: “9186. Dwojra Moskal — Wojsławice”, among the firms struck from the commercial register on 30 March 193933. Five months before the war began.

The sequence is now complete: 1920 registration · 1929–1930 three trades · 1939 struck from the register · 1940 confiscated and placed under German administration · 1942 murder. Five documents, twenty-two years. Each of them is on the documents page.

An address, an owner, and the date of confiscation

And this business has a document. In the confiscation records of the Lublin region, held at the Jewish Historical Institute in Warsaw, there is a single line that closes the question of who "Moskalowa" was8:

Wojsławice, Rynek 123 — the owner: Moskal Dwojra — a shop (sklep) — confiscated on 26 August 1940, and transferred to German management on 8 October 1940 by the Kreishauptmann of the Chełm district. Żydowski Instytut Historyczny, Warsaw, signature 253/2/1472.

The registered owner is Dwojra — Tzvia-Dvora née Alspektor, wife of Judka-Lejba and mother of David — not her husband. She was the one who ran the business, and this fits directly with the "Moskalowa" of the three Polish witnesses. The business stood in the market square itself, at Rynek 123, and the date of its end is known to the day.
